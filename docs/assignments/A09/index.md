## Analyze

For this project, I analyzed the forces and operating conditions of a belt-driven pulley system. I focused on determining the required belt size, shaft diameter, journal bearing dimensions, and frame strength. I used free-body diagrams and engineering equations to evaluate the loads acting on each component.

I began by creating a free-body diagram of the pulley system to identify the tight and slack sides of the belt. I calculated the belt tensions using the friction relationship and determined the minimum belt cross-sectional area needed to safely transmit the load.

## Decide
After completing my analysis, I selected the materials and dimensions for each component based on the calculated stresses, operating conditions, and required factors of safety.

Belt: Selected a 0.250-inch polyurethane belt because it provided sufficient strength while maintaining flexibility.

Belt Length: Selected an adjustable belt length of approximately 19.5 inches to accommodate the pulley spacing.

Shaft: Selected a 0.250-inch steel shaft because it exceeded the required factor of safety under combined bending and torsional loading.

Journal Bearings: Selected oil-impregnated sintered bronze sleeve bearings with a 0.250-inch bore and 0.500-inch length because their pressure and PV ratings exceeded the operating requirements.

Frame: Selected 6061-T6 aluminum because it provided sufficient strength and stiffness while remaining lightweight.

## Communicate

The final pulley system consists of two pulleys connected by a polyurethane belt, supported by steel shafts, bronze journal bearings, and an aluminum frame. I verified that the selected components met the strength requirements and provided adequate factors of safety.

This project helped me better understand how different mechanical components work together in a power transmission system. I practiced using free-body diagrams, belt tension relationships, combined stress analysis, bearing selection, and beam deflection calculations to develop a complete design.

One of the main things I learned was how important it is to consider the forces acting on each component before making design decisions. I also gained more experience selecting standard materials and dimensions based on engineering calculations rather than estimates. Overall, the project strengthened my understanding of mechanical design and how to create a system that is both functional and structurally reliable.
