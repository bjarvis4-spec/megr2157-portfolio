## Analyze

I began by analyzing the input shaft to determine whether its given diameter could safely transmit the required torque. I drew a free-body diagram, calculated the torsional shear stress, and compared the shaft diameter to the minimum required diameter.
 <img width="466" height="504" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/2b21fdef-1a3c-4274-9e95-a01c9bbf3ebe" />

I repeated the strength analysis for the output shaft using its larger diameter and output torque. I calculated the maximum shear stress and minimum required diameter to determine whether the original shaft dimensions were sufficient.
 <img width="595" height="518" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/a045ba63-413c-4253-8bce-ff65d9346f16" />

I calculated the tangential and radial forces generated by the gears to determine the load acting on the journal bearings. I also drew a free-body diagram showing the bearing reactions and calculated the pressure, velocity, and PV rating for the input bearing.
<img width="506" height="639" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/ccffafe8-d842-40e0-ae96-6e9c96364dab" />

I completed the output bearing calculations and compared the results to the operating limits of SAE 841 bronze. I then calculated the bearing wall thicknesses using the selected inside and outside diameters to evaluate suitable bearing dimensions.
<img width="533" height="609" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/137859cd-a7fa-412b-913d-415d796f3101" />

## Decide

I sketched the initial gearbox housing and removable cover to plan how the components would fit together. I included the two gear locations, shaft openings, and four corner fastening holes. This helped me visualize the layout before beginning the CAD modeling process.<img width="614" height="497" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/311e1a7c-054f-4433-9c78-09f35b02f841" />

I created global variables for the output shaft to control its diameter and length. Using parametric dimensions allowed me to maintain the required measurements while making the part easier to modify if needed.
<img width="1440" height="524" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/58b1f3d2-0b08-4034-bd79-11527de4def1" />

I created the output shaft using a circular sketch and an Extruded Boss/Base feature. I modeled the shaft separately so it could be inserted into the gearbox assembly and positioned through the appropriate bearing.
<img width="1218" height="636" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/8ab0b408-1ff7-42c3-a12e-35cc65633e7c" />

I created the smaller input shaft using the same modeling process. The input shaft was designed to fit the pinion gear and rotate within its journal bearings.

 I began modeling the main housing by creating global variables for its overall dimensions, gear spacing, cavity depth, and bearing openings. This allowed me to control important features parametrically and maintain consistent dimensions throughout the model.

## Communicate

Throughout this assignment, I learned how to combine engineering calculations with CAD modeling to design a gearbox. I analyzed the input and output shafts to determine if their diameters were sufficient, then calculated the forces, pressure, velocity, and PV ratings for the journal bearings. I also learned how important it is to select the correct materials and dimensions to ensure the components fit and function properly.

For the CAD portion, I created the shafts, main housing, and removable cover using parametric modeling in SolidWorks. I then assembled the parts with the downloaded gears and bearings to see how everything fit together. Overall, this project helped me better understand mechanical assemblies, the importance of accurate dimensions, and how engineering calculations influence design decisions.
